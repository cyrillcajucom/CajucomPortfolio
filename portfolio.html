Kinsenas

Solo

Kinsenas in 90 seconds, with test invoices.

An AI collections agent built in n8n. It chases unpaid invoices for Philippine small businesses: politely, in Taglish, and timed for payday. Solo project.

What it does

  • Reminders go out 1 to 3 days after payday, the 15th and the end of the month, and never on Sundays or Philippine holidays
  • Gemini writes each email, firmer at each stage, following the owner's notes and the customer's promise history
  • Firmer emails and payment-plan offers wait for one tap in Telegram
  • Reads Taglish replies as paid, promise, dispute or payment plan, and turns "babayaran ko sa Friday" into a real date
  • A promise score for each customer: a broken promise gets chased again
  • Fake-receipt red flags: Gemini reads GCash and bank screenshots and flags reused reference numbers, wrong amounts and old dates
  • An n8n Agent on Telegram answers the owner in Taglish, and asks before it changes anything
  • A daily call list, a Monday summary, and an alert whenever a run fails

Results

  • 5 workflows and 1 n8n Agent, about 48 nodes
  • About 40 test runs with test invoices, covering every path: approve, decline, promise, paid, broken promise, payday, holiday, receipt flags and duplicates
  • Fixed while testing: a table update wiping columns, a hidden tab in a field name, runs on UTC instead of Manila time, and Gemini's free-tier limits

The hardest part

Checking GCash screenshots without trusting them: a screenshot is never proof of payment, so the agent only flags what looks wrong and leaves marking it paid to the owner.

Ascend Hub

Solo

Ascend Hub in 77 seconds, with a real Agent Room session.

A retro orchestrator for AI coding agents, with you in charge of every step. Solo project.

What it does

  • Three AI coding agents plan, build, review and merge as a team in the Agent Room, with a commit and your checks after every task
  • Approval gates: you approve the roles, the plan and the result, and can send a step back with a note
  • Skills: each role gets a library of skills, and the agents open the ones a task needs. The room counts which ones get read and flags the stale ones
  • Skills the agents write: after a session, the orchestrator can turn what it learned into a new skill, safety-scanned before it is saved
  • Subscription limits: a 5-hour and a weekly bar for each agent, and a task moves to another coder when one is nearly out
  • Debates: the agents argue a question, then show where they agree, where they disagree, and a verdict
  • A scorecard of how each agent's work went
  • A shelf of CRT televisions showing every agent's status
  • Between sessions, the same TV plays Spotify, the weather, YouTube and a little game

Results

  • Three AI agents on one task, with three approval gates for you
  • 100 automated test files, across the Agent Room, the status shelf and the media TVs
  • Runs entirely on your computer, on 127.0.0.1 only

The hardest part

Letting three agents edit the same code without breaking it: each session gets its own git branch and worktree, tests run after every task, and nothing merges unless it fast-forwards cleanly.

  • She talks in her own voice, made offline with Kokoro, and listens with faster-whisper on your computer
  • An animated eye that follows your face, reacts to your voice, and shows when she is listening, thinking or asleep

Wake&Brake

Thesis

Wake&Brake, our thesis AVP. 1st place, Overall Best AVP, at CS Expo 2026.

My thesis: a real-time driver fatigue detector on a Raspberry Pi, with an Arduino as the haptic actuator. My role: project manager and main researcher. I led the 4-person team, integrated detection, alerts and the app, and wrote the Arduino alert code.

What it does

  • Face and eye tracking with Haar cascades and dlib landmarks
  • Eye aspect ratio, blink rate, PERCLOS and head posture, to catch micro-sleeps and sustained drowsiness
  • Alerts that escalate through sound, vibration, a buzzer and scent
  • A live feed and stats served locally over Flask, shown in a companion app

Results

  • Tested with 20 drivers in 45 to 60 minute driving-simulator sessions
  • The Pi hands each alert to the serial port in 0.19 ms (median of 110 alerts), 95% of them under 1 ms
  • About 12 frames per second on a Raspberry Pi 4
  • Rated 4.56 out of 5 for reliability and 4.47 for detection by the participants
  • Our AVP won 1st place, Overall Best AVP, at FEU Tech's CS Expo 2026, out of 105 groups and more than 400 students

The hardest part

Alerts that wake you without wearing off: a non-blocking state machine on the Arduino escalates from vibration to buzzer to scent, and a 90-second lockout keeps the scent from spraying so often your nose stops noticing it.

Image Relevance Engine

Capstone

The Image Relevance Engine in 35 seconds, on its own API.

My capstone for the FlyRank AI Backend AI Engineering internship: an API that picks the right photo for an article, and refuses the wrong ones. A local vision model describes each image, embeddings rank them, and a guard says no when nothing fits.

What it does

  • A local vision model (qwen3-vl, through Ollama) describes each image: subject, category, attributes and a confidence
  • Articles and images are compared as embeddings (nomic-embed-text) and ranked by similarity
  • A guard checks the subject, the confidence and the similarity, and explains every rejection
  • Answers "no confident match" instead of guessing when no image fits
  • Reviewers approve or reject suggestions through the API, and an unsafe one can't be approved
  • Background batch jobs with retries, a daily model-call budget, and an alert when an image fails three times
  • A separate database per tenant, versioned SQL migrations, and no paid cloud services

Results

  • Picked the exact labeled image for 7 of 10 articles; 9 of 10 picks were judged relevant on human review. They're two different measures, and neither is 10 of 10
  • Refused a wolf forced onto a fox article, and returned no match for a story about a submarine
  • 40 licensed images, and 21 passing tests

The hardest part

Telling the right picture from a merely similar one: a wolf and a fox sit close together as embeddings, so ranking alone isn't enough. The guard also checks the subject the vision model saw, and refuses when the two disagree.
